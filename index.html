The Map of Philosophy

Reading today's arguments about AI through 2,500 years of philosophy.

67 THINKERS22 DISPUTES12 QUESTIONS

What it is

An interactive map built on one premise: the arguments we are now having about artificial intelligence — whether a machine can think, whether statistics amounts to understanding, who gets to decide what an algorithm optimises for — are not new. They are old questions wearing new clothes.

So the map runs backwards. It starts from a contemporary dispute and traces where it came from, rather than marching forward from the Greeks and hoping the present shows up at the end.

Why it was cut down

It opened with 75 thinkers and 23 questions. It now carries 67 and 12. The reduction was the point, not an accident of editing.

Roughly half of what was here was encyclopedia: dates, schools, three-bullet summaries of what each thinker believed. That layer is now worth close to nothing. Any reader with a language model gets a better version in ten seconds, shaped to the question they actually came with.

What a language model cannot supply is judgment — which two thinkers are genuinely in conflict, why a particular figure still matters, where an idea touches landscape, politics or ecology.

So the encyclopedia layer went and the judgment layer stayed. The same test removed eight names. Kept: people who put forward a claim that can be argued with and shown to be wrong. Removed: forecasts, warnings and positions taken. Two engineers disagreeing about how soon AGI arrives is an industry argument, not a philosophical one.

The 22 disputes

The editorial judgment is easiest to see in the list itself. Each pairing is a claim that these two are genuinely arguing — not merely that they lived near each other.

  1. Heraclitus vs ParmenidesIs reality flux, or permanence?
  2. Plato vs AristotleIs the real in the Forms, or in things themselves?
  3. Confucius vs LaoziGovern through ritual and effort, or through non-action?
  4. Epicurus vs the StoicsIs the good life pleasure, or virtue?
  5. Descartes vs HumeDoes knowledge come from reason, or from experience?
  6. Kant vs MillJudge an act by its motive, or by its consequences?
  7. Hegel vs MarxDoes history move by spirit, or by matter?
  8. Sartre vs AristotleDo human beings have a fixed nature?
  9. Nietzsche vs KantAfter God, where do values come from?
  10. Aquinas vs KierkegaardCan reason reach faith, or does it take a leap?
  11. Turing vs ChalmersIs mind only computation, or something more?
  12. Sartre vs FoucaultIs the self free, or shaped by structures?
  13. Turing vs SearleDoes a machine understand, or only follow rules?
  14. Rawls vs NozickMay the state redistribute in the name of equality?
  15. Popper vs KuhnDoes science advance by falsification, or by rupture?
  16. Chalmers vs DennettIs the hard problem of consciousness a real problem?
  17. Habermas vs FoucaultDoes rational dialogue liberate, or is it itself power?
  18. Kant vs SingerDoes morality extend beyond human beings?
  19. Beauvoir vs ButlerIs gender a situation imposed, or an act performed?
  20. Hume vs PearlIs causation a habit of mind, or a real structure?
  21. Tononi vs DennettIs consciousness integrated information, or a story the brain tells?
  22. Dennett vs BenderDo language models understand meaning, or only predict text?

A note on language. The map itself is written in Traditional Chinese. If you do not read it, a good deal still survives the language barrier: the network view, the dispute pairings above, and the names and dates along the timeline. A full English edition is not planned yet — the content is still moving, and translating it now would mean translating it twice.
